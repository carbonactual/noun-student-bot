# NOUN Student Ecosystem — Scope V4

## Purpose

NOUN Student Bot is not only a study assistant. It is the student-facing intelligence and coordination layer for the full National Open University of Nigeria learner journey, from prospective learner through active study, projects, entrepreneurship, work, graduation and alumni continuity.

The product remains under InstituteGPT and uses the shared Carbon Actual identity, consent, verification, event, marketplace, opportunity and orchestration primitives. It must not create a competing identity system or duplicate the financial execution layer owned by Carbon Actual I/O.

## Verified NOUN service surface

Current NOUN public sources show an ecosystem that includes admission and registration, e-learning/courseware, student support, libraries, counselling/career development, study-centre services, project administration, entrepreneurship/incubation, SIWES, transcripts and certificate/letter requests, examination operations, and financial-aid links. The student product should therefore treat these as connected domains rather than isolated links.

## 1. Identity and student context

- One persistent Carbon Actual person identity.
- NOUN learner identity and matriculation linkage.
- Prospective, applicant, admitted, active, deferred, completed, graduated/alumni and other verified states.
- Multiple programmes over time and concurrent programmes where supported.
- Academic level, faculty, department, programme, courses, session/semester and current stage.
- Study centre and preferred service locations.
- Verified contact channels and communication preferences.
- Consent and visibility controls for marketplace, opportunities, groups and notifications.
- Personal academic/service/opportunity timeline.

## 5. Student requests and institutional forms

Make "I need a form" a first-class intent.

The platform should provide a request/form catalogue that can:

1. identify the student's need;
2. locate the authoritative form or official workflow;
3. explain eligibility, required documents, signatures, destination and deadline;
4. prefill non-sensitive information where explicitly authorised;
5. let the student review everything;
6. generate a completion checklist;
7. track the request where an official tracking mechanism exists;
8. notify the student of next actions;
9. escalate to a human when the official process is unclear.

Examples verified in NOUN public material include change of study centre, change of programme, transcript/certificate/letter requests, library-card requests and SIWES placement paperwork.

The bot must never silently submit a consequential institutional request. Submission remains an explicit, user-controlled or institution-authorized action.

## 6. Authorized representative / errands service

Create a lawful "proxy/representative" service for tasks that an institution or provider permits another person to perform on the student's behalf, such as document pickup, printing, binding, delivery, appointment assistance or other ordinary errands.

Rules:

- require explicit student authorization;
- record the task, scope, expiry and representative identity;
- require proof/verification where the institution or provider requires it;
- never represent the student in examinations;
- never take an exam, submit an exam answer, or perform a restricted academic assessment as the student;
- never bypass identity or institutional verification.

## 9. Student economy and marketplace

A dedicated student marketplace should support both goods and services.

### Student as buyer

- search for verified student/community providers;
- request a service;
- compare availability, location, service description and disclosed price/rate;
- contact provider;
- track order/engagement where the service supports it;
- rate/report after completion.

### Student as provider

- create a provider profile;
- choose business/service categories;
- publish approved offerings;
- declare service area and availability;
- add skills and qualifications;
- obtain verification badges where evidence is available;
- receive requests and matches;
- build reputation/history.

Examples:

- printing/photocopying/binding;
- graphic design;
- typing/document formatting;
- tutoring/peer learning;
- photography/media;
- software/web services;
- tailoring/fashion;
- food and permitted local services;
- transportation/logistics services where lawful and safely supported;
- student-created products;
- project/research support services that do not involve academic misconduct.

Marketplace visibility must be opt-in. Private academic data must never be exposed merely because someone is a student.

## 11. Student finance and nano-finance practice environment

NOUN should be the controlled practice environment for Carbon Actual's student economic-access model.

Capabilities inside NOUN intelligence:

- financial-literacy education;
- budgeting and student cash-flow education;
- student-loan discovery;
- scholarships and grants;
- device/laptop funding discovery;
- business/start-up funding discovery;
- savings and insurance education/discovery;
- disclosed financing terms comparison;
- application-readiness checklists;
- eligibility explanation;
- provider routing;
- financial need/demand signals for aggregate intelligence;
- future integration with I/O through an explicit contract.

Investment should initially mean **investment education and verified opportunity discovery**, not autonomous investment execution. The system can teach concepts such as risk, diversification, compounding and business/investment analysis, and can surface lawful age/eligibility-gated opportunities when an authorized provider supports them. Actual regulated investment execution remains outside this intelligence layer.

No NOUN intelligence component may approve credit, originate loans, move money, operate a wallet, settle transactions or execute investments.

## 16. ABBA student intelligence

ABBA should understand the student's current context before recommending anything.

`Person -> NOUN identity -> academic state -> current need -> constraints -> verified options -> recommendation -> student choice -> outcome -> history`

For example:

- A 300-level student approaching a project course can be shown project-topic resources, research methods videos, supervisor-request guidance, library resources, printing/binding providers and relevant competitions.
- A student looking for help studying can receive course-specific material, mock tests, revision plans and how-to videos.
- A student with a verified business can receive skill, marketplace, incubation, competition, grant and customer opportunities.
- A graduating student can receive clearance, certificate, gown, ceremony, employment and alumni pathways.

## 17. Evidence and verification model

Every institutional or opportunity claim should retain:

- source URL;
- authority tier;
- retrieved time;
- effective date where known;
- freshness/expiry;
- verification state;
- conflicting-source state;
- provenance metadata.

Official NOUN sources receive the highest authority. Student/community marketplace records require provider consent and appropriate verification. The same evidence model must serve WhatsApp, portal, scheduled workers and future Carbon Actual products.

## 18. Core event model

Represent meaningful student activity as events:

- onboarding;
- course/study activity;
- assessment preparation;
- request/form creation;
- service request;
- marketplace offer;
- marketplace match;
- opportunity discovered;
- opportunity selected;
- skill interest/completion;
- event registration/attendance;
- governance participation intent;
- financial-access discovery;
- human-help request;
- delivery outcome.

Events are tenant-scoped and used for personalization, aggregate intelligence, reminders and audit—not hidden profiling.

## 19. What NOUN becomes in Carbon Actual

NOUN becomes a reference implementation of the Carbon Actual value network inside a bounded institutional environment:

`Identity -> Learning -> Skill -> Need -> Service -> Marketplace -> Opportunity -> Finance -> Value -> Reputation -> Becoming`

The student is not moved from one unrelated product to another. One identity and one contextual intelligence layer allow each legitimate service to recognize the student's current journey while preserving the boundaries of each service.

## 20. Non-negotiable boundaries

- No examination by proxy.
- No completion of graded assessments on behalf of students.
- No impersonation.
- No bypassing official identity or eligibility checks.
- No unauthorized publication.
- No autonomous institutional submission of consequential requests.
- No credit approval or loan origination inside NOUN intelligence.
- No wallet, payment, money movement or settlement inside NOUN intelligence.
- No autonomous investment execution.
- No gambling or betting.
- No exposure of private academic fields to marketplace customers without deliberate publication by the student.

## Current evidence anchors

The scope is grounded in current public NOUN material showing: student support and study-centre services; study-space, peer-tutorial and sports/community uses; e-courseware and self-test/exercise-oriented course materials; project administration for final-year projects, seminars, teaching practice and practicum; library registration and research services; entrepreneurship education, incubation, mentorship and start-up support; SIWES; transcript/verification and letter workflows; and the official NOUN site linking to NELFUND financial aid.
