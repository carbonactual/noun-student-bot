# Reducing the Hidden Tax of Time at NOUN
## A proposal from Institute GPT to the National Open University of Nigeria
September 2026

---

## The problem we can help solve

The most expensive thing in higher education is not school fees. It is the time
between knowing and not knowing.

A NOUN student registers, studies, submits assignments, writes exams, logs out —
and then waits:

- Did my result come out?
- Am I missing a requirement?
- Did I fail a prerequisite?
- Is there a problem with my registration?
- Am I still on track for graduation?

Today, the institution knows pieces of the story and the student knows pieces of
the story — but nobody sees the whole story in real time. The cost of that
information delay is measured in repeated semesters, late graduation, and
avoidable attrition. Research on distance learning has repeatedly identified
student support, feedback timeliness, engagement and access to services as the
decisive factors in retention.

Universities measure grades, credits and graduation. What is rarely measured is
where students are losing time. Until lost time can be measured, it cannot be
systematically reduced.

## What we have built

NOUN BOT — a live academic intelligence layer for the NOUN student journey,
built by Institute GPT and powered by ABBA (our AI orchestration engine).

It is not another portal or dashboard. It continuously answers, for each student:
Am I on track? If not, why not? What exactly needs to happen next?

Capabilities live today:
1. Academic guidance grounded in actual NOUN course structure (e.g. DSC 301)
2. Revision and mock-exam practice with instant feedback
3. Semester planning: TMA deadlines, exam registration, requirements tracking
4. Escalation to the right human support when a student needs a person
5. Opportunity discovery: scholarships, internships, research openings

## Why this matters to NOUN specifically

NOUN removed the barrier of geography. Information delay is the new barrier.
NOUN BOT converts the university's fragmented status data into a live timeline
each student can act on — and, in a partnership configuration, can surface
anonymised, aggregate early-warning signals back to study centres and
management: where students are stalling, at scale, before it costs semesters.

## What we propose: a 12-week pilot

Scope: one study centre (or one faculty), 500–1,000 students.

1. Weeks 1–2: baseline measurement — a "lost time" audit: how long students
   wait for status clarity across results, registration, prerequisites
2. Weeks 3–10: students use NOUN BOT as their living academic timeline
3. Weeks 11–12: report — measured reduction in time-to-clarity, support ticket
   deflection, engagement and satisfaction outcomes

NOUN bears no technology cost for the pilot. We provide the platform, support
and reporting.
