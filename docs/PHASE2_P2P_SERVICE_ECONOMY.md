# Phase 2 — Peer Service Economy

## Purpose

NOUN students are not only learners. Many are entrepreneurs, freelancers, tradespeople, professionals and service providers. The intelligence platform must therefore support a consent-based peer service economy alongside academic and financial-access intelligence.

## Core model

A student may publish one or more verified service offerings. A student seeking help may describe a need. ABBA can match the need to eligible providers and prioritize relevant student providers when appropriate.

`Need -> Intent classification -> Location/remote -> Category -> Eligibility -> Provider matching -> Ranking -> Human/customer choice -> Contact/transaction`

## Provider profile

A service-provider profile may include:

- student identity reference
- tenant_id
- service categories
- service description
- skills
- qualifications/certifications where relevant
- portfolio/evidence links
- service area
- remote/on-site availability
- availability schedule
- price/rate or quote model
- response time
- languages
- business name if supplied
- contact channel
- verification status
- trust/reputation signals
- consent status
- active/inactive status

Sensitive student information must not be exposed to customers merely because the student is listed as a provider.

## Matching rules

ABBA should prioritize relevance first, not student status alone.

Ranking signals can include:

1. service/category match
2. required skill/qualification
3. location or remote compatibility
4. availability
5. price fit where known
6. verified status
7. service quality/reputation signals
8. response reliability
9. prior successful fulfilment
10. student-provider preference when relevant

Student status may be a positive ecosystem preference, but must not override qualification, safety, legality, availability or explicit customer requirements.

## Example

A request arrives:

> "I need someone to design a logo for my small business."

ABBA identifies:

- graphic design
- business branding
- likely remote service
- potential small-business customer

It searches the tenant's active service-provider index, including students who have opted into discovery. Relevant student providers can be surfaced first when they meet the matching criteria.

## Provider opportunities

The same engine can match students to:

- tutoring
- graphic design
- web development
- photography
- video editing
- writing/editing
- bookkeeping
- social media management
- fashion/tailoring
- beauty services where appropriate
- repairs/technical services
- consulting
- translation
- research assistance
- logistics
- agriculture-related services
- professional services subject to applicable qualifications

The taxonomy must remain extensible rather than hard-coded to these examples.

## P2P economy boundary

"P2P" has two distinct meanings in this platform:

### P2P services

Student-to-student or student-to-community services. This can be built into the NOUN tenant experience as a marketplace/discovery capability, subject to platform terms, safety, privacy and applicable law.

### P2P finance

Lending, investing, pooled money, wallet transfers and settlement remain in Carbon Actual I/O and require the appropriate regulatory/compliance architecture. The NOUN intelligence layer can discover, educate, match interest and route, but must not silently execute regulated financial activity.

## Safety and trust

The platform must support:

- provider verification levels
- qualification verification where relevant
- reporting
- dispute/escalation flow
- prohibited-service taxonomy
- fraud/spam controls
- rate limiting
- contact privacy
- consent withdrawal
- audit trail
- provider suspension
- customer safety notices

ABBA must never claim a provider is professionally licensed unless that status has been verified from an authoritative source.

## Commercial model

Potential Carbon Actual revenue paths:

- lead/referral fees where lawful and disclosed
- provider subscriptions
- transaction/service fees where appropriate
- institutional marketplace contracts
- premium business profiles
- verified-provider services
- future Carbon Actual marketplace integration

Student providers must be able to participate without being forced into a paid plan.

## Data separation

The marketplace does not become the student's academic record. Academic data should only influence matching when the student has authorized the relevant use or when the information is already intentionally public in the provider profile.

## Future integration

`NOUN Intelligence -> Carbon Actual Opportunity Engine -> Carbon Actual Marketplace -> I/O where payment is required`

The service marketplace and financial marketplace remain separate domains with explicit integration contracts.
